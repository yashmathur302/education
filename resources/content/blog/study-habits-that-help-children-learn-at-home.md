Most children do not need longer study hours. They need a better routine. A few small habits, repeated every day, help children concentrate, remember what they learn and feel less pressure before tests. Here are the habits our tutors recommend most often to parents.

## Fix a regular study time

Children settle into work faster when study happens at the same time each day. Choose a slot that suits your family, such as an hour after an evening snack, and keep it on weekends too, even if it is shorter. Over a few weeks the routine becomes automatic, and you spend less time convincing your child to sit down.

## Create a calm study corner

A small, quiet space with good light and everything within reach makes a real difference:

- A table and chair at the right height, away from the television.
- Books, notebooks, pens and a water bottle ready before the session starts.
- The phone kept in another room unless it is needed for schoolwork.

## Study in short, focused blocks

Younger children can focus for about 20 minutes at a time, and older students for 40 to 50 minutes. Short breaks between blocks help the brain absorb what was learned. A simple timer keeps everyone honest about both the work and the break.

> The goal is not to study more hours, but to make every hour count.

## Review before moving on

Spending five minutes at the end of each session recalling what was learned, without looking at the book, is one of the most effective ways to remember it. Ask your child to explain one idea to you in their own words. If they can teach it, they have understood it.

## Celebrate effort, not just marks

Praise the habit: finishing homework on time, asking a good question, or practising a difficult chapter again. Children who feel their effort is noticed are more likely to keep going when a subject becomes hard.

If your child needs extra help building these habits, a patient tutor can guide them one-to-one until the routine becomes their own.

Board exams are an important milestone, but they do not have to take over family life. With a realistic plan and steady support, most students walk into the exam hall calm and well prepared. These steps help.

## Start with a realistic timetable

Divide the syllabus into small topics and spread them across the weeks before the exam. Leave the final two weeks for revision and practice papers only. A timetable that includes breaks, meals and sleep is far more likely to be followed than one that fills every hour.

## Practise with past papers

Solving previous years' papers under timed conditions shows students the pattern of the exam, the marking scheme and where they lose time:

- Attempt at least one full paper a week in each subject.
- Check answers against the marking scheme, not just the final answer.
- Keep a short list of mistakes and revise it before the next paper.

## Look after sleep, food and exercise

A tired brain cannot recall what it has learned. Seven to eight hours of sleep, regular meals and a short walk or some exercise every day keep energy and concentration steady, especially in the final weeks.

> Calm students remember more. Confidence comes from preparation, not pressure.

## Ask for help early

If a chapter still feels confusing a month before the exam, it is the right time to get help, not the night before. A subject tutor can explain the topic in a different way and give targeted practice until it makes sense.

## Keep conversations positive

Avoid comparing your child with others. Talk about the progress they have made and the plan for the next day. Knowing that their family is behind them, whatever the result, helps students perform at their best.
